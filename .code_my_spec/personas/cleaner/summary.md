# Cleaner (user persona)

## Role
Field cleaning technician — employee or 1099 contractor of a small cleaning company. Works alone or in a team of two, driving between 2–6 jobs a day in residential homes, commercial sites (often nights), or Airbnb/STR turnovers. Interacts with the product almost exclusively through a phone, often while wearing gloves, in and out of cell coverage. This persona is the primary daily USER of the CRM's mobile side.

## Goals
- Know today's plan with certainty: which jobs, what order, addresses, parking, and any mid-day changes pushed loudly — not buried in a group chat.
- Get in the door: entry method, lockbox/alarm codes, pet notes, and emergency contacts reliably attached to each job.
- Know exactly what's in scope: room-by-room checklist per job (fridge? oven? laundry?), so "missed spot" blame lands only on real misses.
- Prove the work: check off tasks, attach before/after photos, report damage or missing items with timestamps.
- Get paid fairly and transparently: clock in/out (or per-job pay) visible per job, drive time accounted for, running total for the pay period.
- Stay safe working alone in strangers' homes: someone knows where they are; a way to flag unsafe conditions.

## Pain Points
- Silent schedule changes: "everything shifts, you forget to text the cleaner" — undelivered assignment changes are the top cause of missed same-day cleanings.
- Access info scattered across texts: "another forgets where the new code is" → lockouts and lost time.
- Scope ambiguity: clients expect thoroughness but included duties are undefined; cleaners get blamed for tasks never agreed to.
- Pay opacity: showing up to a vacationing household and not getting paid; unpaid drive time/gas/supplies in per-job pay models.
- Group-chat operations bury critical details; no single authoritative job brief.
- Supplies surprises: wrong products or broken equipment discovered on site.
- Physical toll and low-status treatment; unrealistic client expectations.

## Context
- Phone-only, often mid-task; needs big targets, offline tolerance, and glanceable job cards. May prefer Spanish or another language.
- Not technical; STR platforms (Turno, Properly) prove photo-checklists work for this population when the UX is dead simple.
- High industry turnover means near-zero onboarding budget: the app must be learnable in one shift.

## Decision Drivers
(Cleaners rarely choose the software, but adoption decides its success.)
- Does it make the day easier in week one: fewer texts, no lockouts, no scope arguments.
- Pay transparency — seeing per-job earnings build during the week is the stickiest feature.
- Respect: an app that treats them as professionals (clear briefs, proof-of-work that defends them in disputes) rather than as GPS dots.
